Case study · Casanostra

Owning things means remembering things

The problem

Dates scattered everywhere

A house or a car comes with a long tail of dates: inspections, insurance, boiler and AC servicing, tax deadlines, repairs that need following up. That information usually lives in someone's head, a paper folder or an old email. Things get missed, and when two people share a home, nobody knows who is handling what.

The product

One place for every house and vehicle

Casanostra keeps tasks, incidents and recurring maintenance per property, with common deadlines suggested by country. You can share a house with a partner or roommate by link or email. It runs in 10 languages, with a free tier and a paid Plus plan billed monthly or yearly.

Engineering highlights
Billing engine
Activation, renewals, refunds and webhooks go through one server-side module. Stripe, Mollie and Revolut sit behind a common provider interface, so switching provider is a configuration change. Plus only activates after the provider confirms payment on the server.
Plans as data
Prices, tiers and house/vehicle limits live in the database, and database triggers enforce the limits. A new paid tier or a sale (like Black Friday pricing) needs no code change.
Invoice integrity
Billing details are copied onto each payment and invoice when they are created, so a customer editing their tax details later never changes a past invoice.
Shared ownership
Invites by share link or email, with checks so the same person can't be invited twice. Row-level security in Postgres decides who sees what.
Automation & admin
Scheduled jobs handle renewals and reminders. A separate admin back office checks the admin role on the server before every privileged read.
